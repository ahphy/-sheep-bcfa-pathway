Figure X | Host synthesis of mutton odor-associated fatty acids. Rumen fermentation produces acetate, propionate and butyrate, which undergo epithelial absorption. Acetate supplies carbon for adipose lipogenesis; propionate is mainly taken up by the liver for gluconeogenesis, while butyrate can be metabolized to beta-hydroxybutyrate in the epithelium. Cytosolic propionyl-CoA and butyryl-CoA supply to adipocytes remains unresolved; the diagram does not imply direct transport of rumen CoA esters. ACC generates malonyl-CoA from acetyl-CoA; its side reactions yielding methylmalonyl-CoA and ethylmalonyl-CoA are proposed donor sources requiring validation. The shared FASN model comprises initial extension, branch introduction, a further extension and hydrolytic release. Each extension involves condensation, reduction, dehydration and reduction with NADPH. Malonyl-, methylmalonyl- and ethylmalonyl-CoA retain two, three and four carbons after decarboxylation, respectively. The final extension adds two carbons at the carboxyl end, shifting the branch from C2 to C4. R denotes the primer hydrocarbon group and X denotes the methyl or ethyl substituent; neither label represents a single fixed carbon atom. Only three selections are shown: R=CH3/X=CH3 yields MOA (4-methyloctanoic acid, C9H18O2); R=CH2CH3/X=CH3 yields MNA (4-methylnonanoic acid, C10H20O2); R=CH3/X=CH2CH3 yields EOA (4-ethyloctanoic acid, C10H20O2). No fourth arbitrary R/X combination is implied. Solid, dashed and dotted lines indicate general biochemical/physiological foundations, candidate reactions/assembly and unresolved supply, respectively. ACC side reactions, FASN acceptance of branched donors, early short-chain release and complete in vivo flux in sheep remain unverified. Product stereochemistry is unspecified. ACC, acetyl-CoA carboxylase; FASN, fatty acid synthase; CoA, coenzyme A; ACP, acyl carrier protein.
